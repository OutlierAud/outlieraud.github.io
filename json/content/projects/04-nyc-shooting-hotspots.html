When I started looking at shooting incidents in New York City, I wanted to know when they peaked and where they clustered. Then I wondered what would happen if I put those two questions together. Would the same areas stand out if I looked only at the busiest hours?

I used the NYPD's historical shooting records from 2006 to 2024. I counted each incident once, even when it involved more than one victim, and used the same set of records with coordinates for both the time analysis and the maps.

How I approached the analysis

What stood out?

Recorded shootings declined for much of the period, then nearly doubled from 777 in 2019 to 1,531 in 2020. The count stayed elevated through 2022 and fell to 869 in 2024. Looking at boroughs separately helped me see how differently that rise and fall played out across the city.

What changed when I narrowed the window?

Late Friday and Saturday nights stood out on the weekday-by-hour chart. When I mapped those hours using the same grid and colour scale as the all-hours map, many of the same concentrations remained visible, particularly in parts of Brooklyn, the South Bronx and northern Manhattan.

These maps pool 19 years of records. They show where recorded incidents accumulated. I'd need population or other exposure data to compare risk, and a year-by-year look to see whether the concentrations shifted.
