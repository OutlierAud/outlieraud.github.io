Did countries that reported their first COVID-19 case later see slower growth in those first few weeks? I wondered whether a later start might give countries more time to prepare, and whether I could see any trace of that in the case records.

Using Johns Hopkins University data covering 197 countries, I measured how long it took each country to report its first case. Then I calculated the average daily growth in confirmed cases over its own first 45 days. That gave each country the same window to compare.

Did the latecomers have an advantage?

Countries that reported their first case later tended to record slower growth. In the fitted regression, a first report 100 days later corresponded to an average daily growth rate about 2.2 percentage points lower. The slope still pointed down when I removed the seven countries with zero recorded growth over their first 45 days.

About 21% left me with plenty of questions. A first reported case gives me a date I can compare across the dataset, but testing and reporting affect when that date falls and how many cases are counted afterwards. Public-health measures also varied across countries. The pattern gives me a reason to ask what countries did with the extra time. Answering that would take evidence about what each country actually did.
