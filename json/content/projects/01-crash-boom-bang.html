Okinawa, Japan. A subtropical paradise of winding coastal roads, island adventures, and hidden stories. But when it comes to road safety, a few concerns often surface: wet and slippery roads, reckless driving, and the high number of accidents along Route 58.

But how much truth lies behind these perceptions?

To find out, I used SQL and data visualisations to explore 2,749 police-reported crashes involving injury or death that occurred in Okinawa Prefecture in 2024. I wanted to see where crashes clustered, when they peaked, and what stood out when I looked specifically at fatal crashes. The records come from Japan’s National Police Agency.

What stood out?

Route 58 recorded 322 crashes, more than any other route code in the dataset. Together, the Central and South regions accounted for 84.1% of the recorded crashes.

Weekday crash counts peaked around 8 a.m. and 5 p.m.

A different pattern emerged when I looked at severity. 4.14% of crashes in full darkness were fatal, compared with 1.04% in full daylight. Crashes between vehicles were the most common overall, while pedestrian–vehicle crashes made up the largest group of fatal crashes, with 17 of 43.

These findings give me a few places to look more closely, including Route 58, nighttime crash locations, and pedestrian crossings. Traffic volumes and pedestrian counts would help put the crash totals in context.

I documented the analysis in six notebooks and brought the findings together in an interactive D3.js executive report.
